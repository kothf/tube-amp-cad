How it is computed

The loop is broken at one end of the chosen part and measured by Middlebrook's double injection: a voltage injected in series, then a current injected into the junction, combined into the gain once around the loop. That is exact whatever the impedances either side of the break. The signal generators are quiet (shorts behind their output resistance); every part is linearized at the idle operating point, as for the frequency response.

The generator drives the circuit with 1 V at each frequency; every part is linearized at the circuit's idle operating point (all generators off), so this is the small-signal response. Tubes include their interelectrode capacitances (Miller effect); transformers their inductance and leakage. Large-signal effects (clipping, transformer saturation) show on the oscilloscope and the spectrum analyzer instead.
